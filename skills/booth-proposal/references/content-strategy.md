# Content strategy – what the booth says, and where

Clients hand over decks, brochures, product sheets and posters full of text and numbers. A visitor walking past reads perhaps a headline and a number. This file is the method for finding the few things worth putting on the walls, writing them at the right length for each viewing distance, and placing them where the design carries them. It applies to any client and any booth.

Content is design material, not an afterthought: the most distinctive facts often inspire the insight and the concept itself.

## 1. Inventory: extract every candidate

Read every client file, including text inside images, posters, charts and maps. Dense slides hide the best facts in small print: when text is small, render the page at a higher resolution and read it in parts. Copy wording exactly; logos and calligraphic lettering are easy to misread, so mark anything uncertain "verify spelling".

Record each candidate as one content item:

```
| ID | Type | Text (verbatim) | Source | Flags |
| C1 | proof | … | brand-deck.pdf p.4 | superlative · number |
```

Types:
- **proof** – a fact that proves something: a number, a share, a ranking, an award, a "first", "only" or "largest"
- **story** – a dated milestone or an origin story
- **product** – a product's name, what it is for, what makes it different
- **brand line** – slogan, tagline, mission, values
- **entity** – names of sites, subsidiaries, partners, people
- **action** – what visitors are asked to do (scan, play, take, meet)
- **mandatory** – logos, legal lines, regulatory statements the client requires

Flags: `number`, `superlative` (first, only, largest, leading), `comparative`, `regulated` (health, medical, financial, environmental claims), `dated` (a figure that may be out of date), `verify spelling`, `needs translation`.

Never add facts that are not in the files. If the brief needs content the client has not supplied (for example product claims for a product that is only named), list it under "Content to request from the client".

## 2. Select: find what is most representative

Score each item from 1 to 3 on five questions:

| Criterion | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Distinctive | Could only this client say it? (The no-swap test, applied to words.) |
| Proof | Is it concrete and verifiable rather than an adjective? |
| On idea | Does it support the insight and the concept's idea? |
| For this audience | Does this event's audience care about it? (Regulators, buyers, scientists and consumers care about different things.) |
| Short | Can it be said in a few words without losing its meaning? |

The most representative content scores high on Distinctive and Proof together. "Committed to quality" scores low on both; a first, an only, a dated origin, a specific share of a market score high. Ties go to what serves the concept's idea.

Items flagged `superlative`, `comparative`, `regulated` or `dated` can be used, but they go on a list of **claims for client sign-off**: exact wording, source, and a note that the client must confirm the claim is current and approved for public display.

## 3. Hierarchy: write for viewing distance

The higher the level, the fewer the words. One idea per surface.

| Level | Read from | Job | Length guide |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L1 Landmark | 10 m and more, from the aisle | Brand and one headline that carries the idea | One headline for the whole booth, about 3–8 words (about 4–12 Chinese characters) |
| L2 Invitation | 3–5 m | The few proof points that make people step in | At most three, each a short phrase or a number with a label |
| L3 Discovery | 1–2 m, inside | Products, milestones, names, how things work | One topic per panel, a heading plus at most 40–60 words |
| L4 Depth | In hand or on screen | Full stories, specifications, films, quizzes | Screens, printed material, QR codes, staff conversation |

Rules of thumb:
- Big numbers make excellent L2 content: a number with a two-to-four-word label reads faster than a sentence.
- Long lists (all products, all sites, all milestones) belong in L3 or L4, or become a designed element: a sequence of names, a timeline in the architecture, never a dense panel.
- A common signage rule of thumb is about 25 mm of letter height per 3 m of comfortable reading distance (1 inch per 10 ft). A headline meant to stop people at 10 m needs far more. The designer sets final sizes.
- For an international audience, decide one primary language per level; the secondary language sits smaller beneath it, never at equal size everywhere.

Write the copy by shortening the client's own words. Keep the meaning, keep numbers exactly, and keep regulated claims verbatim. Mark every line you shortened as "edited from C<n>" so it can be traced.

## 4. Place: let the design carry the content

Content belongs in the architecture, not on posters added to it. Before assigning surfaces, ask how the concept's hero can carry the most important content: dates engraved on a sequence of frames, names on the bays of a wall, a single number built into the counter. Then place the rest:

- L1 where the hero and the brand are seen from the aisle
- L2 at the invitation zone, at eye level or on the hero itself
- L3 on the product and discovery surfaces, between 1.0 and 2.0 m above the floor
- L4 on the screen loop, the interactive quiz (questions drawn from L3 and L4 content), printed takeaways and QR codes

Keep at least half of the booth's visible surfaces free of text. If the content does not fit, cut content, not space.

## 5. Output

**Message platform** (shared by all concepts, built in step 3):

```
Headline options (L1): three alternatives, each with the content items it draws on
Key messages (L2): the three strongest, each traced to content items
Discovery topics (L3): the list of topics worth a panel
Depth content (L4): what goes to the screen, the quiz and the takeaways
Claims for client sign-off: exact wording, source, flag
Content to request from the client
```

**Content map** (one per concept, built in step 4 on the final geometry):

```
| Surface | Level | Copy (draft) | From | Note |
| Hero: <element> | L1 | … | C3, C7 | carved / printed / light |
```

In the concept presentation, show only "What the booth says": the headline and the three key messages in one or two lines. The full inventory, the content map and the sign-off list go to the appendix.
