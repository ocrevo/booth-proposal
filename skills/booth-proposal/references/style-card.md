# Style card – template and rules

A style card is a short note on what a reference booth tells you about the client's taste: what makes it theirs, what they love, the finish they expect, the energy they are used to. It is a note for the designer, not a filter. **The reference images themselves go to the image model with every View 1 prompt** (references/image-prompts.md); the card never replaces them, it is not written into the prompt, and nothing the client likes in them is turned into a rule against it. The prompt takes only one line from it: what to keep and evolve, and what not to copy.

Where a reference's execution can be done better, the Elevate line suggests how, but the concept keeps what the client loves – the brightness, the glow, the brand colour, the scale – unless the idea needs something else.

## Rules

- Look at every reference image yourself. Describe only what is visible. If a photo is dark or partly blocked, say so instead of guessing.
- Name what makes it the client's own: its signature structure (a portal, an arch, a wall), its colours, its light, its brand presence.
- Give colours as approximate HEX values and name where each is used. Mark them "≈" because colours in photos shift with lighting.
- Several photos of the same booth make one card; different booths make different cards.
- If the reference booth is the client's own previous booth, say so: its brand elements and signature structure can be reused and evolved. At least two of the three concepts grow out of that structure, each pushed in a different direction and made clearly more premium; the third may break away (references/art-direction.md).
- Do not copy, from any reference: other companies' logos and wordmarks, slogans and text, posters and roll-up banners, and another company's signature pieces or exact layout. The user may move items between Keep and Do not copy.
- Without references there is no style card: the design territories in art-direction.md suggest each concept's design language.
- Add an Elevate line only where an execution detail would look cheap in a render (a messy cable of light, a crowded wall of text); keep the intent and the energy.
- List the reference files to attach to the View 1 prompts.
- Judge the client's register from the references: **restrained** (few materials, quiet light, little text), **balanced**, or **expressive** (strong colour, bold graphics, light effects, slogans). It sets how far the concepts may go: an expressive client shown three restrained concepts will find them "too plain", so at least the concepts that evolve their reference keep its energy (colour, scale, brand presence) while their execution is elevated. Record the risk in the critique.

## Template

```
STYLE CARD S1 · v1 · Source: <file names>
Whose booth: <client's own previous booth | another company | unknown>

What makes it theirs: <the 2–4 signature elements: structure, colours, light, brand presence>
What the client probably loves: <the intent worth keeping>
Register: <restrained | balanced | expressive> – <one line of evidence>
Colours: <role – name ≈ HEX – where it appears>, …

Keep and evolve: <what the concepts grow from, e.g. the rounded portal arch, the blue glow>
Do not copy: <…>
Elevate: <reference does> → <elevated execution>, e.g. LED strips outline the arch → one continuous cove of indirect light inside a matte arch

Attach to View 1 prompts: <file names>
```
